Why institutions under-invest in the commons

Each institution chooses to cultivate shared open infrastructure or to harvest it (paper, section 9). A maintained commons benefits everyone once enough contribute, so there are often two stable outcomes, and the better one still falls short of what would serve everyone best. Recognition and funding change which outcome holds.

Privacy that holds wherever a case comes from

Clinical cases teach because they are real, and real cases identify people. Removing a name is not enough: age, dates, diagnosis, place and an unusual history can identify a patient together. The tools here follow the strictest of the major frameworks so they hold in every country: the EU GDPR, the US HIPAA Privacy Rule, the UK Data Protection Act 2018, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and ICMR guidelines, Brazil's LGPD, South Africa's POPIA and Kenya's Data Protection Act, with the Declaration of Helsinki (2024), the CIOMS guidelines (2016) and WHO guidance on AI for health (2021). Where two of them differ, the stricter requirement applies. The health IT notebook shows each step on a synthetic case.

How this was published

  1. Seal. Every file is listed with its SHA-256 hash in a manifest that must verify before anything leaves the author's machine.
  2. Clear. The repository's own gate checks the tests, the parity of the two models, the notebooks, the references, the trade-mark marking, secrets and file sizes.
  3. Attest. The author types his intent (PUSH, then MINT, each with a reason). Each typed intent is kept as a receipt and timestamped with OpenTimestamps.
  4. Mint. Misty DOI deposits the sealed commit on Zenodo and the DOI is written back into the README and the citation file, and sealed again.

Notebook 06 walks you through the same process for your own teaching material, under your own account.
